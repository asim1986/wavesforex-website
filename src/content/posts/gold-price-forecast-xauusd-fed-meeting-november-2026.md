Gold prices staged a notable recovery in US trading on Friday, climbing back into the **$3,640–$3,670 per ounce** range after dipping below $3,500 earlier in the week. While the bounce has offered relief to bullion investors following a steep 35% correction from January's record highs near $5,608/oz, Wall Street analysts largely view the move as a short-term technical rebound rather than the start of a sustained bull run.

## Why Sustaining the Rally Faces Steep Hurdles

Several macroeconomic and institutional headwinds in the United States continue to weigh on gold’s upside potential:

*Photo: [Karolina Bobek on Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/photos/treasury-building-in-washington-dc-2Xndqkf2BMI) · [Unsplash License](https://unsplash.com/license).*


- **Hawkish Federal Reserve Expectations:** Upwardly revised Q3 US GDP growth (4.3%) and persistent Core PCE inflation (3.3% year-over-year) have pushed CME FedWatch probabilities to over 90% for a 25-basis-point interest rate hike at the December 8–9 FOMC meeting. Higher Treasury yields increase the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding assets like bullion.
- **US Dollar Strength:** Although a slight pullback in the US Dollar Index (DXY) toward the 104.00 mark gave gold room to breathe on Friday, elevated US yields continue to support the greenback, keeping dollar-denominated commodities under pressure.
- **Institutional Positioning & Liquidity:** Periodic selloffs in US tech and AI equities have forced institutional funds to liquidate gold holdings to raise cash. Reflecting near-term caution, major desks such as Standard Chartered recently downgraded their gold outlook from *Overweight* to *Neutral*.

## Key US Technical Levels to Watch

From a technical standpoint, spot gold (`XAU/USD`) established a near-term floor after testing 52-week lows between $3,427 and $3,500. However, bulls face heavy overhead resistance:

| Technical Metric | Price Level (USD / oz) | Market Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Immediate Resistance** | $3,697–$3,700 | Key ceiling; requires a daily close above to shift momentum |
| **Current Trading Zone** | $3,644–$3,671 | Driven by month-end short covering and bargain hunting |
| **Near-Term Support** | $3,595 | First line of defense on pullbacks |
| **Critical Floor** | $3,427–$3,503 | Breakdown below risks a deeper slide toward $3,340 |

## The Long-Term Case: Eyes on a 2027 Fed Pivot

Despite near-term turbulence, longer-term institutional forecasts remain constructive. Analysts at J.P. Morgan note that a 20% decline in crude oil and gasoline prices since October, combined with tighter financial conditions, should eventually cool inflation and prompt the Federal Reserve to pivot toward rate cuts in the second half of 2027. Under that scenario, a weaker US dollar could propel gold back toward **$4,500–$4,600/oz** by Q4 2027.

## What’s Next for Gold Traders?

In the immediate term, US gold markets are expected to remain choppy and range-bound below **$3,700/oz**. Because a December rate hike is already heavily priced in, traders are looking past the rate decision itself toward **Fed Chair Jerome Powell’s December 9 press conference and the updated 2027 "dot plot"** for signals on when the central bank will pause its tightening cycle.
